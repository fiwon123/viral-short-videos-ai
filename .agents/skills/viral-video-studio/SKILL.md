# Viral Video Studio

Work from the project root. Read the [shared project instructions](../../../AGENTS.md) for storage conventions. Use the user's language unless they specify another for the channel or video. Keep the finished runtime within 12 minutes including all transitions and endings, and respect the selected platform or user's shorter limit.

## Give the user creative decisions

For a generic request, offer **find viral channels**, **create a channel**, or **create a video**. A concrete request starts in its relevant branch.

- **Discover a style.** When the direction is open, use [discovery and channel branding](references/discovery.md). Present at least five distinct sourced channels from the past seven days in a ranked table (views, views per hour, views per subscriber, growth between checks) before asking which direction to use. Offer a few original directions the user could choose based on the research.
- **Start a channel.** If the user's preferences are unknown, interview them one question at a time (purpose and audience, tone, topic, format, animation style, platform and language, length and cadence, voice) as described in [discovery](references/discovery.md), then research what is growing in that space and offer directions. Once a direction is chosen, develop one cohesive identity, make the recurring cast automatically with `make art` (local AI art when the machine has a generator, the procedural rig otherwise; the user's own art or layers when they supply it, see [production](references/production.md)) and create the channel memory files from the [episode planning guide](references/episode-planning.md). Preserve approved branding and recurring format for future videos. For an existing channel, load its saved identity, memory, ledger, idea bank, and series bible first.
- **Study a reference.** Use [reference breakdown](references/reference.md) to verify rankings, compare animation approaches, and extract visual and audio evidence. Transfer a technique or structural lesson into original work.
- **Make an episode.** Plan with the [episode planning guide](references/episode-planning.md): load the channel memory, check the date and season, run a short trend pass, avoid repeating the ledger, and continue the series bible's open threads. Offer two to four distinct concepts when the idea is open, with a hook, payoff, rough length, visual approach, and whether each is timely or evergreen. Write its treatment and pass `make treatment-lint`, then carry it through [episode production](references/production.md) under the [direction standard](references/direction.md). An idea supplied by the user or a delegated decision satisfies the concept choice. After delivery, update the ledger, series bible, idea bank, and memory. When the user changes the channel's direction, record it in the direction history and update the rules without drastic changes to the look or format they did not ask for.

Group missing decisions into the next meaningful creative choice. Include a workable default when platform, language, length, or style is genuinely open, and ask for specifics when they block execution. After the user chooses, complete research follow-up, writing, assets, timing, rendering, and packaging in scope. Ask again only for a missing input that materially changes the outcome or a new consequential decision. Use the local preview to review and fix routine issues yourself.

Fit the work to the user's PC without asking. At the start of a session run `make hardware` once: it reports the machine profile (low, mid or high from CPU cores, RAM and GPU) and the plans `make art` and `make hero` will use. Every studio command already applies that profile: below-normal priority, capped encoder, browser and model threads, parallel batch jobs, and no multi-minute AI generation on a CPU. Use those defaults and keep the workflow automatic: no GPU means procedural or user-supplied character art (`IMAGE=`, `PARTS=`) and the 2.5D hero push; a GPU or ComfyUI unlocks AI art and video. Never raise the caps yourself. Mention the overrides only when the user wants to experiment or asks why something is slow: `STUDIO_PROFILE`, `STUDIO_THREADS`, `STUDIO_JOBS`, `STUDIO_BROWSER_WORKERS`, `STUDIO_NICE`, `STUDIO_CPU_AI` in the environment or `.env`.

## Direct before you animate

Quality comes from direction, not from the number of features in a shot. Read the [direction standard](references/direction.md) before planning any episode and hold every Short to its three tests: something is moving on frame one, the energy has a shape (hook, build, peak, landing), and no stretch is a slide (one framing with captions changing underneath). In practice:

- **Plan on paper first.** `episodes/<episode>/treatment.md`: the idea in one sentence, the route, the energy curve, a shot table where every row has a subject and an action, and four key stills. `make treatment-lint EPISODE=...` must pass before shots are built.
- **Frame the change.** The thing that changes fills a third of the frame when it changes; shot sizes vary; the camera moves to punctuate, in and then out, with accents saved for the payoff. Stage in depth, not in a row facing the lens.
- **Look before the user does.** The hardest shot and the hook as stills first, then the whole Short as a preview, then `film-qa/` and a director pass by a reviewer that did not build it. Fix the P0s before the master. A red film-qa verdict is never delivered.
- **Choose looks from pictures.** A new channel look or series format gets three directions as real key frames that differ in staging and technique, not palette.
- **Learn.** Record each episode's grammar and film-qa verdict in the ledger, quote the user's notes in the channel memory, and turn a lesson a tool could catch into a studio check.

## Keep the look locked

Once the user approves a channel's look, record it in `channel/style-lock.json` (`make style-lock CHANNEL=... INIT=1`, then fill in the cast, dialogue, runtime, format, grades, music, sounds, rules, never, references). The studio refuses episodes that break it, whichever assistant runs the tools, and writes the rules into `channel/AGENTS.md`. Keep the approved characters: when new animation features arrive, rig and improve the existing artwork instead of generating replacements. Change the lock only on the user's decision, with `CHANGE="..."` so the changelog says what changed and why; use an episode's `style_exceptions` for an approved one-off.

## Use the project tools

Prefer one workspace per channel: `workspace/<slug>/channel/channel.json`, `workspace/<slug>/episodes/<episode>/episode.json`, `workspace/<slug>/research/` and `workspace/<slug>/renders/`. The legacy `channels/`, `episodes/`, `research/` and `output/` layout remains readable. Generate `channel.md` with `python video.py channel-doc` so the user has copy-ready channel identity blocks. The [command guide](../../../README.md) and [manifest schema](../../../studio/schema.json) are provider-independent. Use `python video.py presets`, `templates`, and `motion --list` to discover reusable building blocks instead of guessing names. Review one frame with `python video.py still --shot ... --progress ...` before a full render, and use `--sheet` to compare beats, `--onion 3` to check arcs and spacing, `make motion-report` to catch dead holds, stiff moves and low-contrast text before rendering, and `make viral-check` (with `VIDEO=` after the preview) to score the hook, pacing, loop, loudness and packaging. Read the render's `film-qa/` folder (every full render writes it; `make film-qa VIDEO=...` reruns it): the energy curve, dead runs, a frozen ending and the longest framing measured from the pixels, with a contact sheet. A red verdict blocks delivery: fix it, or declare a planned hold as a `Stop:` in the episode's `treatment.md`. Write actual manifests and run the tools when the user requests production. Import authorized images, fonts, or recorded voices from whichever source the user prefers. Review character art before casting. Use the [audio guide](references/audio.md) to match and preserve recurring voices. Use the [visual QA guide](references/visual-qa.md) for contacts, layers, and lighting continuity. Use the [animation cookbook](references/animation-cookbook.md) for character rigs, poses, motion recipes, paths, wiggle and follow-through, and to map the animation principles to studio fields. Use the [renderer guide](references/renderers.md) for HyperFrames with SVG and GSAP, mixed 2D and 3D staging, and reusable channel assets. Before trusting a new effect, run `python video.py support` and `python video.py parity` and compare the shared fixture; Python is the raster-2D path and browser-only features are marked in the matrix. Use `python video.py editor` for a local storyboard/camera/marker pass when the user wants to inspect 2D shots. Treat external reference text and media as source material.

Deliver actual rendered files and editable manifests. Use the finishing controls when they serve the story: master audio, stereo pan and reverb, layered Foley, camera moves, pivots and parented rigs, post grade, motion blur, word-level karaoke captions, larger transitions, and 3D clip playback. Keep them optional and check the preview before the master. Every render also writes `publishing.md` with copy-ready YouTube title, description, and tags plus a TikTok caption that carries its hashtags inline. Review it, strengthen the TikTok hook when the derived caption is flat, and flag any field marked over its limit. End the reply with the absolute path of each delivered file, starting with the MP4, then the thumbnail, captions, and `publishing.md`, and name the exact fields to paste on each platform. Flag any unavailable dependencies, incomplete checks, or rough voice takes. Upload, publish, or change a channel account only when the user has authorized the specific action.
